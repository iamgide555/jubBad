# Engine evidence questions (2026-09-25)

G4–G8 came from a code audit, not observed session failures. They are
hypotheses to investigate with real-session evidence, not scheduled fixes.
G1–G3 were resolved and remain recorded in the
[archived feature review](archive/2026-09-21-feature-review-and-roadmap.md).

Record anonymized session context and the observed pairing/queue outcome
before proposing a rule change. Use measurements or simulation where noted,
but do not commit identifiable player or session data.

- [ ] G9. Balanced mode trades partner variety for rating balance by design (`BALANCE_WEIGHT = 2`, additive with the 10:1 partner/opponent weights, not lexicographic like variety). Simulated over 8 nights (14 regulars, 3 courts, 30 seeded runs): repeat-partner games 17-25% in balanced versus 0% in variety, even with well-seeded ratings; with an unseeded all-1200 start it pays that cost for little balance until about night 4. Small groups (8 players, 2 courts) reach 85-95% repeats against a variety floor of 60%. Needs real-session evidence of "same partner again" complaints in สูสี before touching the weight; a confidence-scaled balance term (weight by games played) is the candidate fix. Prototype: not committed (scratch simulation, see the 2026-10-19 revision in `docs/2026-10-06-pricing-tiers.md`).
  - 2026-10-28 check (see G10): with ratings fed to the engine, nothing about the *scale* of the rating gap moves the trade-off. Stretching gaps ×2 or ×3 (equivalent to a 2–3× `BALANCE_WEIGHT`) left true imbalance unchanged within noise, while repeat partners rose from about 4% to 5–9%. With perfect skill information the engine reaches near-zero imbalance at 12–25% repeat partners, so the variety cost is inherent to balancing accurately, not a miscalibrated weight. Still no real-session complaint to act on.
  - 2026-10-28 small-roster check (evenly spread ratings 900–1600, 200 runs per row, the real `generateRound` with the server's inputs, courts rotating independently; script `engines/evidence/small-roster-sim.mts` on branch `proto/balance-tolerance-band`, commit 871f178). **Balanced mode does not match สลับคู่ on partner variety.** At 20 matches (a real night averages about 24), for mixed rosters (9–11 players on 2 courts, 13–15 and 17–18 on 3) the most-repeated partner pair partners 3.0–4.9 times in balanced versus 1.5–2.3 in variety, with 1.6–5.0 pairs partnering 3+ times versus about none; at 5–6 players it is far worse (a pair partners 12 and 8 times in 20 matches versus 4 and 3). The fairness bought is real: the average team rating gap is about 60–67 points versus 180–195 in variety. Who plays is still chosen by games then wait (games-played spread stays at 1.5 or less), so the repeats come only from how the chosen four are split. Same-opponent repeats of 3+ are common in *both* modes at these roster sizes (10 players over 20 matches is 80 opponent pairings across 45 pairs), so no pairing change can hold opponents to 1–2.
  - Two levers were prototyped (opt-in, off by default, 408 engine tests unchanged). A **tolerance band** (a court's gap only counts above N points) is a dial, not a fix: variety gained trades against fairness almost one-for-one, and a tolerance of 600 reproduces variety exactly. A **partner cap of 2** (a pair that has already partnered twice is charged heavily for a third time, balanced mode only) is much cheaper: for the mixed rosters above the worst pair partners 2.0–2.1 times at a gap of 76–98 points, against 1.8–2.4 times at a gap of 131–136 for a tolerance of 300. It does not help 5–8 players (a cap of 2 is only reachable for 10 matches at 5 players, since 10 pairs × 2) or the lock-in rosters in G12, and it must count partnerships *per session* (under the all-time history switch it would saturate). Not adopted: no real สูสี session exists to show the repeats actually bother a host.
- [x] G10. Is Elo's constant (K = 16) or logistic scale (400) mis-tuned? — resolved 2026-10-28, no change. Two real groups were replayed read-only from a production backup (group A: 20 players, all tagged with a level, 163 decisive doubles matches over 7 sessions; group B: 18 players, none tagged, 142 decisive doubles matches over 7 sessions). Singles (22 matches, group B only) were too few to test.
  - **Replay prediction.** Scoring each match with the rating from before it: higher K improves log-loss steadily (K16 → K128: 0.477 → 0.426 in A, 0.616 → 0.514 in B) with a peak around K 128–192, but accuracy stays flat (A 78–84%, B 70–73%) and the final player ordering barely moves (Spearman 0.92 between K16 and K64). K 128 moves a rating about 42 points per match and spreads a group over 800–1800 after seven nights, which is a form tracker, not a rating. A decaying schedule (K from 128 down to 8–32 by games played) did no better than a constant K, and cold-start matches stay near coin-flip at every setting. Favourites win far more often than the 400-point scale predicts (predicted 78%, actual 98% at a 70%+ favourite in A), so probabilities are underconfident, but Elo is only used for *ranking* a gap against partner variety.
  - **Pairing simulation.** Fitted each group's true skill (regularised, sd about 230–290 points), then ran the real `generateRound` in balanced mode over 8 nights × 8 rounds × 3 courts with 14 players and 8 seeds, drawing outcomes from true skill while the engine saw only Elo. Score is the mean `|P(A wins) − 0.5|` per game by true skill (0 = perfectly fair): variety 0.23–0.26; current balanced untagged 0.15–0.17; K 16/32/64 and gap ×1/×2/×3 all 0.14–0.18, so none is distinguishable from noise (8 seeds, no confidence intervals); tagged with ±75-point noisy level seeds on an uncompressed scale 0.06 (an optimistic assumption, see the real-tag check below); oracle true skill 0.01.
  - **Real tag accuracy (2026-10-28, group A only, the one tagged group).** Each player's level seed was regressed on skill fitted from their results: seed ≈ 1130 + 0.45 × fitted skill, so one level step (100) stands for about 220 points of real skill, and levels *compress* the true spread. Residual sd is 84 points; after removing the fit's own bootstrap uncertainty (36) the tag noise is about 76 points. Spearman between seed and fitted skill is 0.58 (coarse levels tie heavily). **All 20 tags were set after the host had watched the player (3–32 games first, median about 14); none was set before a player's first match**, so 76 is a floor for an a-priori tag. Re-running the pairing simulation with this structure: true imbalance 0.130 (group A's skills) and 0.111 (group B's) against 0.170 and 0.148 untagged, an improvement of about 25%, not 60%. With tag noise 120 it is about even with untagged (0.165 / 0.138), and at 170 it is *worse* than untagged (0.195 / 0.159). With realistic tags K 32 was slightly better than K 16 (0.121 vs 0.130, within noise).
  - **Conclusion.** Leave K, the 400 scale and `BALANCE_WEIGHT` alone. What limits balanced mode is rating information, not how Elo is tuned: tagging levels as accurately as group A's hosts did (after watching play) cuts true imbalance about 25%, and noisier tags lose the benefit entirely. Neither group has ever run a balanced session (all 14 sessions were variety), so Elo was display-only there and this has not been observed in real play. The "pro farms noobs" worry is not borne out: a 1500 beating a 900 gains about 0.5 points and loses about 15.5, and over 7 sessions the strongest players' ratings rose by 94 points (A, 82% win rate) and 225 points (B, 80% win rate) rather than running away.
  - **Reproducing.** The scripts are in `engines/evidence/` (README there); they run against a copy of a production backup and take group codes as arguments.
  - **Caveats.** The simulation plays whole rounds, not independently rotating courts; true skill is fitted from about 160 matches per group; tag accuracy was measured on one group (20 players) and assumed for the other; group A's tags all post-date watching play, so a-priori tags are probably noisier than measured.
- [ ] G11. Suggest a level from rating once a player has enough games, for groups that do not tag levels. G10 found tagging levels is the largest lever on balanced mode (about 25% less true imbalance with group A's tag accuracy, nothing if tags are noisier than about 120 points) and group B had tagged none, while its ratings after seven sessions already separated players well (among players with 30+ doubles games, win rates ran from 31% to 80% and ratings from 1094 to 1425). Open design questions: how many decisive games before suggesting, whether the host confirms (levels are host-only judgements, a rating-derived level must never apply silently), and how the suggestion interacts with a level reset (setting a level resets the rating to its seed from that moment — `RatingAnchor` in `engines/elo.ts`). Needs a decision on whether untagged groups are common enough to justify it.
- [x] G12. Rosters where exactly 0 or 4 players sit out (8 players on 2 courts or 1, 12 on 2, 16 on 3) rotate through a few fixed quartets when courts finish one at a time. Resolved 2026-10-28 as a known limitation with a host workaround, no code change. Mechanism: when a court frees while the other is still playing, the only players free to be re-mixed are the four who just finished plus the four who sat, and sit-out selection (games played, then wait) runs before the split is chosen, so the four who sat are always the next four. In the small-roster simulation (G9) that left each player partnering only 1.1, 1.7 and 1.9 distinct people at 20 matches in balanced mode (8, 12 and 16 players) against 3.0–3.8 in variety, and at 30 matches the worst pair partnered 13.2, 9.0 and 6.9 times in balanced (5.2, 3.9 and 3.0 in variety).
  - **Workaround: wait until every court is idle, then propose.** `propose` already plans across all idle courts and commits the one asked for, so nothing needs building; the cost is that the first court to finish waits for the other. Re-planned together over 14 rounds (evenly spread ratings, one seed, `walkthrough` method in `docs/reports/2026-10-28-pairing-modes-11-and-15-players.md`): 12 players on 2 courts, worst pair 2 times in both สลับคู่ and สูสี (55 and 45 of 66 partner pairs); 16 players on 3 courts, worst pair 1 and 2 times (84 and 78 of 120); 8 players on 2 courts, สลับคู่ meets all 28 pairs (worst pair 2, which is arithmetic: 56 partnerships over 28 pairs).
  - **Still open at exactly 8 players in balanced mode:** with only 8 players balance dominates, so สูสี partners 16 of 28 pairs and repeats one pair up to 4 times even with courts re-planned together. Accepted; it is the roster-size limit, not selection.
  - Not seen in a real session; the simulation is one seed for the re-planned runs against 200 for the one-court-at-a-time figures.
